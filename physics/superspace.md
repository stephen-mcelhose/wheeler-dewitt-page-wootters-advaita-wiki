# Superspace and Minisuperspace

Superspace is formally the quotient \(\mathrm{Riem}(\Sigma)/\mathrm{Diff}(\Sigma)\), the space of spatial geometries rather than bare spatial metrics. A [minisuperspace](../definitions/def-minisuperspace.md) is a symmetry-reduced submodel of this formal configuration space. It is infinite-dimensional and has global and singular-stratum subtleties. The DeWitt supermetric is indefinite, so the Wheeler–DeWitt equation is not an ordinary Schrödinger equation on a positive-definite configuration space.

Minisuperspace restricts to highly symmetric degrees of freedom. It is valuable for explicit examples, but results from a truncation must not be presented as results for full general relativity. No minisuperspace equation, lapse/rescaling relation, or source locator is introduced here: those remain deferred pending the separate direct source-and-convention review.
